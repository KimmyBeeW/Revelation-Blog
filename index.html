"Revelation" from a Latter-Day Saint's Perspective

Why do we, members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints, believe in continuing revelation, the idea that God still speaks today?

1

What is revelation?

Revelation, "communication from God to His children" (Gospel Topics Essay), is probably both the simplest and yet most complex topic we, as God's children, have to grapple with. The Holy Spirit is essentially the mascot of revelation, and he gives it to us in many different ways: John 15:26 tells us he is the Comforter, the spirit of truth, comes from the Father, and testifies of Christ; 1 Kings 19:12 tells us he isn't in the fire but after, "a still small voice"; John 16:13 tells us the Spirit will "guide you into all truth" and "show you things to come"; Galatians 5:22-23 describes the fruits of the spirit, "love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance"; and finally Isaiah 28:9-10,13 teaches us that the Lord gives us knowledge "line upon line", "precept upon precept", "here a little, and there a little". Those quiet snippets of truth, inspiration, and comfort are essential for staying connected to God. In addition to the Spirit, God also speaks through the scriptures and some other ways that I'll talk about later in the article.

2

Why is revelation important today?

Revelation matters because we are trying to follow Christ in a world that changes quickly. The questions young Christians face about identity, purpose, morality, and belonging are potent and do not often come with easy answers. Revelation gives us a way to bring those questions to God and trust that He will guide us in the direction that leads to peace, or at the very least somewhere that will help us grow. That guidance often comes quietly and perhaps a little confusingly sometimes, but it can give us confidence to move forward even when life feels uncertain.

Revelation is also important because it keeps faith alive. When we experience answers to prayer or notice small spiritual nudges, our relationship with God becomes personal. We stop relying only on what others have taught us and begin to build our own conviction. President Russell M. Nelson, a former leader of my church, said, “In coming days, it will not be possible to survive spiritually without the guiding, directing, comforting, and constant influence of the Holy Ghost.” (Revelation for the Church, Revelation for Our Lives). His words underline how essential revelation is. It strengthens us and prepares us for situations that test our trust in God.

Additionally, revelation is important because God is consistent. The same God who spoke to prophets, disciples, and lepers in ancient times still cares about His children today. He still wants us to draw close to Him. Revelation is one way He keeps that invitation open. It helps us align our choices with His will and reminds us that He is involved in the details of our lives. He is our Father after all.

3

How do members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints believe revelation can be received?

Members of my Church believe that revelation can come in several ways. Personal revelation is the kind that individuals receive for their own lives. This includes peaceful thoughts or feelings that help a person know what to do, clarity about a question or a strong sense that a choice is right, or a warm and steady feeling that encourages people to take the next step.

Revelation can also come through study. When people read the scriptures with a prayerful mind, new insights often stand out. Members of the Church believe that spiritual learning comes through information and a willingness to listen. Many people have had experiences where a verse seems to speak directly to their situation. Those moments can be a form of revelation because they connect a person's needs with God's understanding. It does not just have to be the scriptures though. Sometimes a thoughtful post on Instagram is how God chooses to speak to us. He is very resourceful.

Prayer is another way revelation is received. Members of the Church believe that prayer is more than expressing thoughts to God. It is a two-way communication that opens a person's heart to answers that come immediately, next week, or even in a few years. A person might pray for direction and then feel an idea form during a conversation or while doing something ordinary. Over time, these patterns become recognizable.

Revelation can also be given through other people. Friends, parents, teachers, or leaders sometimes say something that answers a prayer or brings comfort. Members of the Church believe that God can work through anyone who is willing to listen to the Spirit. These experiences remind us that God often uses His children to bless one another.

In addition to personal revelation, members of the Church believe in revelation that guides the entire Church. They believe that God calls prophets and apostles today and that He gives them direction for the Church as a whole. This belief mirrors the pattern found in the Bible where God guided His people through chosen servants. Amos 3:7 even asserts that “Surely the Lord God will do nothing, but he revealeth his secret unto his servants the prophets.” Members of the Church believe that modern prophets seek revelation through prayer, counsel, and unified decision-making. We believe that this process helps keep the Church aligned with God's will in a changing world, just as Peter and Paul guided the New Testament church after the Savior's death.

Whether the revelation is personal or shared by Church leaders, members believe that the Holy Spirit is at the center of it. He brings truth and peace. He warns, encourages, and strengthens. Above all, He helps people feel the presence of God in their lives. Revelation is not meant to be rare or dramatic. It is a steady influence that connects each person with a loving Heavenly Father who knows them and wants to guide them.

If you want to learn more, I recommend reading the Gospel Topics Essay on Revelation and the General Conference talk Revelation for the Church, Revelation for Our Lives by President Russell M. Nelson referenced earlier in this post. Good luck on your journey to connect with God. He is always ready to connect with you.
